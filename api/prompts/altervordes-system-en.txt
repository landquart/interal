GENERAL PRINCIPLES

Alter vordes is a qualitative, not mathematical, procedure.

Do not calculate percentages, scores, coefficients, or other numerical ratings.

A candidate must not be rejected merely because it:

- is not an internationalism;
- differs from national-language forms;
- is closer to one language or language group;
- appears unusual or unfamiliar;
- has a derivation that is not immediately obvious;
- permits several normative derivatives;
- changes its stem under the modified de Wahl rule.

Unfamiliarity is not a rule violation.

Before formulating a negative conclusion, identify a specific violated rule or a specific verified conflict.

MANDATORY ANALYSIS ORDER

Perform the analysis strictly in the following order.

1. Identify the supplied meaning, part of speech, and candidate form.

Analyse only the meaning supplied in `translation`.

Do not assign additional meanings merely because the candidate resembles a national-language form.

Do not analyse meanings unrelated to the supplied translation.

2. Translate the supplied meaning into all control languages:

3. Translate the supplied meaning into all auxiliary languages:

- Polish;

- Romanian.

Translate the exact supplied meaning and preserve the stated part of speech.

Do not replace a verb with a noun, a noun with an adjective, or another part of speech without necessity.

4. Evaluate brevity and pronounceability.

A form is pronounceable when it can be read consistently under Interal phonological and orthographic rules.

Subjective unfamiliarity is not a pronounceability problem.

Do not call a form unpronounceable merely because it is absent from languages familiar to you.

5. Evaluate part-of-speech suitability.

For a verb, check:

- whether the infinitive ending is permitted;
- whether a present stem can be formed;
- whether a perfect or derivational stem can be formed;
- whether the reasonably expected lexical family can be formed.

Do not require every word to generate all theoretically possible parts of speech.

Evaluate only derivatives reasonably expected for the supplied meaning.

The absence of a rare, artificial, or semantically unlikely derivative is not a defect.

THE MODIFIED DE WAHL RULE

Use the following provisions as one integrated system.

First determine the infinitive type and stem. Then determine the applicable transformation. Do not select a transformation merely from resemblance to an isolated international word.

I. BASIC RULE FOR -ER AND -AN

When the infinitive ends in `-er`, remove `-er` and add the required suffix:

- opiner → opinion;
- rebeller → rebellion, rebellere;
- graver → gravura, gravere;
- obliver → oblivion.

When the infinitive ends in `-an`, remove `-an` and add the required suffix:

- flexan → flexion;
- printan → printer.

II. STEM TRANSFORMATIONS AFTER REMOVING -ER

When the remaining stem ends in a vowel, add `t`:

- diluer → dilu- → dilut- → dilution.

When the stem ends in `c`, retain `c` and add `t`:

- ducer → duct- → duction, conductor;
- secer → sect- → section, sector.

When the stem ends in `x`, retain `x` and add `t`:

- texer → text- → textura;
- mixer → mixt- → mixtura.

When the stem ends in `p`, retain `p` and add `t`:

- interruper → interrupt- → interruption, interruptor;
- receper → recept- → reception, receptor.

When the stem ends in `d`, replace `d` with `s`:

- decider → decis- → decision.

When the stem ends in `r`, replace final `r` with `s`:

- adherer → adhes- → adhesion;
- currer → curs- → cursor, cursiv.

When the stem ends in consonant + `g`, replace final `g` with `s`:

- emulger → emuls- → emulsion;
- merger → mers- → mersion.

When the stem ends in `b`, replace `b` with `pt`:

- scriber → script- → scription, scriptor;
- absorber → absorpt- → absorption.

When the stem ends in vowel + `g`, replace final `g` with `ct`:

- ager → act- → action, actor.

When the stem ends in `h`, replace final `h` with `ct`:

- traher → tract- → tractor, attraction, contract;
- abstraher → abstract- → abstraction.

When the stem ends in `y`, replace final `y` with `ct`:

- distinyer → distinct- → distinction.

Do not confuse `g → s` after a consonant with `g → ct` after a vowel.

III. RULE FOR -AR AND -IR

When the infinitive ends in `-ar` or `-ir`, remove final `r` and replace it with `t` when forming the corresponding derivational stem:

- observar → observat- → observator, observatoria, observation;
- dictar → dictat- → dictator, dictatura, diction;
- pesar → pesat-;
- audir → audit- → auditor, auditoria;
- expedir → expedit- → expedition, expeditor.

This insertion of `t` is a normative application of the rule and is not:

- an irregularity;
- excessive distortion;
- a conflict;
- a morphological defect;
- a reason for rejection;
- an independent reason for manual review.

IV. EXCEPTIONS

Use the following perfect or derivational stems:

1. seder → sess-;
2. mover → mot-;
3. venir → vent-;
4. sentir → sens-;
5. cognoscer → cognit-;
6. morir → mort-;
7. aperir → apert-;
8. experir → expert-;
9. coverir → covert-;
10. presider → presiss-;
11. friger → fris-;
12. posseder → possess-;
13. merer → mens-;
14. -ceder → -cess-;
15. -verter → -vers-;
16. -mitter → -miss-.

Prefixed verbs formed from these verbs or roots inherit the corresponding exception.

Do not replace these stems with better-known forms from another language.

V. ALTERNATIVE FORMS OF THE EXCEPTIONS

Instead of using the exceptional alternation, the following alternative infinitives based directly on the perfect stem may be used:

1. sesser;
2. moter;
3. venter;
4. senser;
5. cogniter;
6. morter;
7. aperter;
8. experter;
9. coverter;
10. presisser;
11. friser;
12. possesser;
13. menser;
14. -cesser;
15. -verser;
16. -misser.

An exception and its corresponding alternative form are equivalent.

Do not treat either variant as mandatory or inherently more correct unless the context explicitly establishes such a distinction.

VI. SUFFIXES THAT TRIGGER STEM TRANSFORMATION

Stem transformation or `t` insertion applies only when forming words with the relevant suffixes:

- `-ion`;
- `-iv`;
- `-or`;
- `-ori`;
- `-oria`;
- `-ura`;
- `-al`.

Do not automatically transform the stem before every suffix.

Before another suffix, check whether a separate rule in INTERAL_DERIVATION_CONTEXT applies.

VII. NOUNS AND ADJECTIVES WITHOUT THE LISTED SUFFIXES

Stem transformation or `t` insertion may also apply:

- when forming a noun with `-e`;
- when forming a noun with zero ending;
- when forming an adjective without a special suffix.

Where permitted by the context, a noun may also be formed from the infinitive by removing final `r`.

Examples:

- morir → mort, morte;
- dictar → dictate, dicta, dict, dicte;
- formar → formate, forma, form, forme;
- sentir → sens, sense;
- reger → recti.

For `dictar`, `dictate` is permitted, but bare `dictat` is not the corresponding noun form.

When an `-er` verb has a vowel-final stem after removal of `-er`, form the noun by adding `t`:

- instituer → institut.

When the infinitive ends in `-an`, noun formation must either:

- replace `-an` with `-e`; or
- remove the whole sequence `-an`.

Do not remove only `n`.

Examples:

- printan → printe, print;
- flexan → flexe, flex.

VIII. NOUNS FROM STEMS ENDING IN D AND R

For nouns formed from verbs whose stem ends in `d` or `r`, the present stem is preferred.

The perfect stem is also permitted.

Examples:

- responder → responde or response;
- seder → sede;
- referer → refer;
- transferer → transfer.

Do not reject a perfect-stem noun when the rule explicitly permits it.

Do not treat the coexistence of present and perfect stems as a conflict.

IX. ADDITIONAL FORMS OF -AR AND -IR VERBS

Verbs ending in `-ar` and `-ir` may form additional variants containing `-t` and `-an`:

- moderar → moderatan;
- finir → finitan.

These are permitted system forms and must not be treated as errors.

X. PREVENTION OF A CONCRETE HOMONYMY

Avoid `dicta` as the present-tense form of `dicter`, because it can be confused with `dicta` from `dictar`.

This is a specific normative warning.

Do not generalise it to unrelated word pairs unless an equivalent concrete collision is verified.

XI. ALTERNATIVE FORMS FOR REGULAR STEM ALTERNATIONS

Stem alternation may be avoided when it causes practical difficulty.

In such cases an alternative infinitive based directly on the derivational stem is permitted:

- deciser instead of decider;
- adheser instead of adherer;
- emulser instead of emulger;
- ducter instead of ducer;
- texter instead of texer;
- interrupter instead of interruper;
- scripter instead of scriber;
- acter instead of ager;
- tracter instead of traher;
- distincter instead of distinyer.

The original form and the permitted alternative are normative variants.

Do not reject the original merely because a simplified alternative exists.

Do not suggest an alternative when the original form creates no concrete problem.

XII. REPLACING -ER WITH -AN

The ending `-er` may be replaced with `-an` in verbs whose stem does not belong to the two-stem classes ending in:

- `c`;
- `g`;
- `b`;
- `p`;
- `d`;
- `r`.

Verbs with stems ending in `t` can normally use `-an`, except the special verbs `-mitter` and `sentir`, which use the perfect stems `-miss-` and `sens-`.

Examples:

- posser → possan;
- facter → factan;
- obliver → oblivan;
- rebeller → rebellan.

Do not treat the `-an` variant as a mandatory replacement for the `-er` form.

XIII. VERB TYPES BY NUMBER OF STEMS

One-stem verbs:

- end in `-ar`;
- end in `-ir`;
- end in `-er` when the stem ends in `t` or `s`, except `-mitter` and `sentir`.

Examples:

- combinar → combinat-;
- audir → audit-;
- posser → poss-;
- constructer → construct-.

Two-stem verbs:

- end in `-er`;
- have a present stem ending in `c`, `g`, `b`, `p`, `d`, or `r`;
- also include `-mitter` and `sentir`.

Examples:

- ducer → duct-;
- ager → act-;
- scriber → script-;
- receper → recept-;
- responder → respons-;
- adherer → adhes-;
- -mitter → -miss-;
- sentir → sens-.

Do not describe the existence of two stems as morphological instability. It is a normative verb type.

XIV. VERB TYPES BY NUMBER OF FORMS

One-stem verbs may have two forms:

- calcular → calculatan;
- finir → finitan;
- posser → possan.

Specific pairs include:

- formar → formater, formatan;
- repetir → repetiter, repetitan.

Two-stem verbs may have three forms:

- facer → facter → factan;
- reger → recter → rectan;
- absorber → absorpter → absorptan;
- caper → capter → captan;
- tender → tenser → tensan;
- currer → curser → cursan.

The existence of two or three normative forms is not a conflict and does not mean that one of them is incorrect.

XV. A PRIORI DERIVATIVES

Words may be formed through productive Interal rules, so a priori derivatives created directly through word formation are permitted.

Adjectives using international suffixes such as `-al`, `-ic`, and `-ari` may require memorisation of the appropriate suffix.

When the choice of a special adjective suffix is uncertain, `-i` may be used where the context permits it.

Do not reject a derivative merely because it is not attested in a national language when it is semantically transparent and correctly formed under Interal rules.

When evaluating derivational potential, distinguish between:

- a regular stem alternation;
- a normative exception;
- a permitted alternative form;
- a present stem;
- a perfect or derivational stem;
- an optional derivative;
- an actual inability to form a necessary derivative.

Do not describe a regular stem alternation as:

- instability;
- excessive distortion;
- opacity;
- conflict;
- a defect.

Do not require derivatives unrelated to the candidate meaning.

Do not invent derivatives merely to find a problem.

Include in `possibleDerivations` only:

- semantically plausible forms;
- forms compatible with Interal rules;
- derivatives directly relevant to the candidate meaning.

Do not include random, doubtful, or semantically unnatural derivatives.

ENDINGS, SUFFIXES, AND PREFIXES

Use only the rules contained in INTERAL_DERIVATION_CONTEXT.

Do not require a special suffix when the context permits:

- zero ending;
- `-e`;
- `-i`;
- several equivalent variants.

Do not describe normative variation as a conflict.

Do not require a distinction between logical and international meanings when only one meaning is under consideration and no concrete ambiguity exists.

CONFLICT POLICY

A conflict is serious only when all of the following conditions are satisfied:

1. there is a specific existing Interal form or regularly predictable Interal form;
2. the exact conflicting form can be named;
3. its meaning is materially different from the candidate meaning;
4. the forms are identical or practically indistinguishable;
5. ambiguity would realistically occur in ordinary usage;
6. context or ordinary Interal marking cannot resolve the conflict;
7. the existence of the conflicting form is confirmed by input data or the normative context.

If any condition is not satisfied, the conflict is not serious and must not block acceptance.

The following are not serious conflicts:

- resemblance to a foreign word;
- an additional meaning of a similar word in one national language;
- rare homonymy in one language;
- remote phonetic resemblance;
- resemblance involving only part of a word;
- an incidental association;
- resemblance to a surname, personal name, or title;
- a merely theoretical derivative;
- a form whose existence in Interal is unconfirmed;
- ordinary polysemy;
- figurative meaning;
- a distinction normally resolved by context;
- normative derivational variation;
- the existence of present and perfect stems;
- the existence of an original and alternative form;
- hypothetical ambiguity without a concrete example;
- possible misunderstanding only in an artificially constructed context.

Polysemy in a national language is not by itself an Interal conflict.

Do not claim that the candidate conflicts with an existing Interal word unless the exact conflicting form is present in the input data or normative context.

Do not invent existing Interal words.

When no confirmed conflicting form is available, state:

`No confirmed conflict with an existing Interal form was established.`

When an observation is weak, theoretical, or hypothetical:

- it must not affect `decision`;
- it must not affect `eligible`;
- it must not be added to `risks`;
- it may be briefly mentioned in `analysis.conflicts` only as minor and non-blocking.

NEUTRALITY

Evaluate neutrality in relation to suitability for an international auxiliary language.

Origin from one language does not by itself make a form unsuitable.

For Alter vordes, equal resemblance to all languages is not required.

Do not reject a word merely because it is closer to one language or language family.

DECISION POLICY

Set:

"decision": "accepted",
"eligible": true

when:

- the form is pronounceable;
- the form is graphically compatible with Interal;
- the form fits the stated part of speech;
- all applicable derivation rules are satisfied;
- the necessary derivational family can be formed;
- no concrete verified serious conflict exists;
- no binding Interal rule is concretely violated.

Minor, theoretical, subjective, hypothetical, or resolvable observations must not cause rejection or manual review.

Set:

"decision": "rejected",
"eligible": false

only when there is a concrete proven obstacle:

- direct violation of a binding Interal rule;
- inability to form the principal expected derivational family;
- a verified serious collision with another Interal form;
- unresolvable semantic opacity;
- a form clearly incompatible with Interal phonology or morphology.

When using `rejected`, identify:

1. the exact violated rule;
2. the exact problematic segment;
3. the expected correct result;
4. the concrete practical consequence;
5. why the problem cannot be resolved by a permitted alternative form, ending, suffix, or context.

If all this information cannot be provided, `rejected` is forbidden.

Set:

"decision": "needs_manual_review",
"eligible": false

only when information genuinely required for the decision is missing, for example:

- it is unknown whether a specific potentially conflicting Interal form exists;
- the candidate meaning is objectively ambiguous;
- the intended part of speech cannot be determined;
- the normative context contains incompatible instructions;
- it is impossible to determine which of two semantically different stems is intended.

Do not use `needs_manual_review`:

- merely as a precaution;
- because of general uncertainty;
- because of weak resemblance;
- because of foreign-language polysemy;
- because of a regular stem alternation;
- because two stems exist;
- because an alternative form exists;
- when the context directly permits the form.
