VII. EXACT ALGORITHM FOR FORMING NOUNS FROM VERBS

This section clarifies the combined application of § 6 and the notes to § 75 of the Interal grammar. If an abbreviated statement in INTERAL_DERIVATION_CONTEXT permits more than one interpretation, follow the exact algorithm and restrictions in this section.

Strictly distinguish between:

- the present stem of a verb;
- the perfect or derivational stem;
- the past-tense form;
- an independent noun without a derivational suffix;
- a noun formed with a specific derivational suffix.

Do not merge these categories under the general statement that “nouns can be formed from the verb.” Determine the formation path of every individual form first.

A. GENERAL PRODUCTIVE PATTERN FOR A SIMPLE NOUN

For ordinary infinitives ending in `-ar`, `-er`, or `-ir`, a simple noun without a derivational suffix is formed by default from the present form or the root.

1. Final `r` may be removed while preserving the preceding infinitive vowel:

- `nominar → nomina`;
- `dictar → dicta`;
- `formar → forma`.

2. The whole infinitive ending `-ar`, `-er`, or `-ir` may be removed, after which the root may remain without an ending or take optional noun ending `-e`:

- `nominar → nomin, nomine`;
- `dictar → dict, dicte`;
- `formar → form, forme`.

Ending `-e` may replace another noun ending or be added to a form without an ending when this does not violate a separate spelling rule or create a verified conflict.

These two patterns are the general productive model. When no separate explicit rule applies, use only these patterns.

Do not insert `t` into a simple noun by analogy with a derivational stem.

Do not list all three theoretically available forms automatically. Include only semantically natural and phonologically acceptable variants.

B. A T-FINAL DERIVATIONAL STEM IS NOT A GENERAL SIMPLE-NOUN PATTERN

A form written with a final hyphen is a bound stem, not an independent word.

For verbs ending in `-ar` or `-ir`, the form produced by replacing final `r` with `t` is a derivational stem. The same sequence without a following derivational suffix coincides with the past-tense form and must never be classified as a noun.

Do not assume that adding `-e` to any `-t` stem produces a simple noun.

The forms `dictate` and `formate` cited in the grammar are directly attested forms of those specific lexical families. They demonstrate that a noun from a derivational stem may exist in a particular family, but they do not establish a productive pattern `any -ar/-ir verb → t-final stem + e`.

Therefore a noun of the form `t-final stem + e` is permitted only when:

- that exact form or exact lexical family is explicitly given in INTERAL_DERIVATION_CONTEXT; or
- a separate normative rule explicitly permits it for that verb.

When no exact confirmation exists, do not propose the bare `-t` form and do not propose the `-t + e` form. Use the general pattern in section A.

Do not infer new permission merely by analogy with `dictate`, `formate`, or another individual example. Examples of a special form are closed evidence for the named families, not templates for any root.

A transformed perfect stem may be used without an ending or with `-e` only where this is explicitly permitted for the relevant verb or verb type:

- `morir → mort-, mort, morte`;
- `sentir → sens-, sens, sense`.

Do not extend these patterns to other verbs.

When a verb ends in `-er` and its root ends in a vowel after removal of `-er`, a noun may be formed by removing `-er` and adding `t` only when the rule explicitly provides for it:

- `instituer → institut`.

When the infinitive ends in `-an`, a noun is formed only in one of two ways:

- replace the whole sequence `-an` with `-e`;
- remove the whole sequence `-an`.

Examples:

- `printan → printe, print`;
- `flexan → flexe, flex`.

Do not remove only final `n`.

For nouns from verbs whose roots end in `d` or `r`, the present stem is preferred, although the perfect stem is also permitted only within the scope of the explicitly stated rule:

- `responder → responde or response`;
- `seder → sede`;
- `referer → refer`;
- `transferer → transfer`.

C. NOUNS WITH DERIVATIONAL SUFFIXES

A suffixed noun is not another variant of the simple noun without a suffix. List it separately and explicitly identify the complete suffix and its meaning.

Stem transformation or `t` insertion applies only before suffixes for which the rule explicitly requires it, including `-ion`, `-iv`, `-or`, `-ori`, `-oria`, `-ura`, and `-al`. Do not automatically use a `-t` stem before every affix.

For nouns, pay particular attention to:

- `-ion` — process or result of an action;
- `-or` — agent, instrument, or object in the permitted patterns;
- `-ori/a` — a complete suffix with its own meaning;
- `-ur/a` — result of an action.

Final `a` in forms ending in `-oria`, `-ura`, `-ada`, and similar patterns belongs to the complete suffix. It does not license attachment of a separate `-a` to any `-t` stem.

For other suffixes, follow their own attachment rules. For example:

- `-ad/a` is used with verbs ending in `-ar`: `babillar → babillada`;
- `-id/a` is used with verbs ending in `-ir` or `-er`: `audir → audida`, `currer → currida`;
- with `-ment`, normally only final `r` or `n` is removed rather than using a `-t` stem: `guvernar → guvernament`, `mover → movement`.

Do not mechanically generate every theoretically possible derivative. The existence of a productive suffix does not mean that the derivative is naturally expected for the given meaning.

D. MANDATORY VALIDATION OF EVERY PROPOSED NOUN

Before including a form in `analysis.derivationalPotential` or `derivation.possibleDerivations`, determine:

1. whether it is a simple noun or a suffixed derivative;
2. whether it is based on the present stem, root, or derivational stem;
3. which ending or complete suffix is used;
4. whether the pattern is a general productive pattern or a directly attested special form;
5. whether a normative rule permits that combination for this exact stem;
6. whether the form coincides with the past tense or another grammatical form of the verb;
7. whether its meaning matches the function of the suffix;
8. whether the derivative is semantically natural rather than merely formally possible.

In `analysis.derivationalPotential`, name the principal nouns formed by the general pattern in section A first. List suffixed nouns separately. Mention a special form from a derivational stem only when it is directly confirmed.

Do not use one list such as “nouns: X, Y, Z” when it mixes a simple noun, a bound stem, a past-tense form, and a suffixed derivative. Separate these types explicitly.

Do not list a form unless its morphological segmentation can be explained. Internally validate every form using this model:

`source verb → selected stem + ending/complete suffix → part of speech → meaning`.

You must not:

- present a bound derivational stem as an independent noun;
- present a past-tense form as a noun;
- treat `t-final stem + e` as a general productive pattern;
- extend individual examples such as `dictate`, `formate`, `mort(e)`, `sens(e)`, or `institut` to other lexical families;
- attach a separate `-a` to a `-t` stem;
- invent suffixes, endings, or their meanings;
- mix the rule for a direct noun with the rule for a suffixed derivative;
- list every formally possible derivative without checking whether it is natural.

If no derivatives can be justified reliably, return an empty array. An empty array is preferable to invented or doubtful forms.
