VII. EXACT ALGORITHM FOR FORMING NOUNS FROM VERBS

This section clarifies the combined application of § 6 and the notes to § 75 of the Interal grammar. If an abbreviated statement in INTERAL_DERIVATION_CONTEXT permits more than one interpretation, follow the exact algorithm and examples in this section.

Strictly distinguish between:

- the present stem of a verb;
- the perfect or derivational stem;
- the past-tense form;
- an independent noun without a derivational suffix;
- a noun formed with a specific derivational suffix.

Do not merge these categories under the general statement that “nouns can be formed from the verb.” Determine the formation path of every individual form first.

A. INDEPENDENT NOUN WITHOUT A DERIVATIONAL SUFFIX

For ordinary infinitives ending in `-ar`, `-er`, or `-ir`, the grammar permits several equivalent methods of noun formation.

1. Remove only final `r`, preserving the preceding infinitive vowel:

- `nominar → nomina`;
- `dictar → dicta`;
- `formar → forma`.

2. Use the root without the infinitive ending and either leave it without an ending or add optional noun ending `-e`:

- `nominar → nomin, nomine`;
- `dictar → dict, dicte`;
- `formar → form, forme`.

Ending `-e` may replace another noun ending or be added to a form without an ending when this does not violate a separate spelling rule or create a verified conflict.

These forms are based on the present form or the root. Do not insert `t` unless a separate derivational-stem pattern is being used.

B. NOUN FROM A PERFECT OR DERIVATIONAL STEM

A form written with a final hyphen is a bound stem, not an independent word.

For verbs ending in `-ar` or `-ir`, the form produced by replacing final `r` with `t` is a derivational stem. The same sequence without a following suffix or ending coincides with the past-tense form and must not be classified as a noun.

When an independent noun without a derivational suffix is formed from such a stem, ending `-e` is added:

- `dictar → dictat- → dictate`, not `dictat`;
- `formar → format- → formate`, not `format` as a noun specifically formed from stem `format-`.

Do not add ending `-a` to a derivational stem ending in `-t`. The sequence `t-final derivational stem + a` is not a general noun-formation pattern. A form ending in `-a` is produced under the first pattern by removing only final `r` from the infinitive, not by attaching `-a` to a `-t` stem.

A transformed perfect stem may be used without an ending or with `-e` only where the rule for the relevant verb type explicitly permits it:

- `morir → mort-, mort, morte`;
- `sentir → sens-, sens, sense`.

Do not generalise zero-ending nouns of this type to all verbs ending in `-ar` or `-ir`.

When a verb ends in `-er` and its root ends in a vowel after removal of `-er`, the noun is formed by removing `-er` and adding `t`:

- `instituer → institut`.

When the infinitive ends in `-an`, a noun is formed only in one of two ways:

- replace the whole sequence `-an` with `-e`;
- remove the whole sequence `-an`.

Examples:

- `printan → printe, print`;
- `flexan → flexe, flex`.

Do not remove only final `n`.

For nouns formed from verbs whose roots end in `d` or `r`, the present stem is preferred, although the perfect stem is also permitted:

- `responder → responde or response`;
- `seder → sede`;
- `referer → refer`;
- `transferer → transfer`.

C. NOUNS WITH DERIVATIONAL SUFFIXES

A suffixed noun is not another variant of the simple noun without a suffix. List it separately and explicitly identify the suffix and its meaning.

Stem transformation or `t` insertion applies only before suffixes for which the rule explicitly requires it, including `-ion`, `-iv`, `-or`, `-ori`, `-oria`, `-ura`, and `-al`. Do not automatically use a `-t` stem before every affix.

For nouns, pay particular attention to:

- `-ion` — process or result of an action;
- `-or` — agent, instrument, or object in the permitted patterns;
- `-ori/a` — a complete suffixal form with its own meaning;
- `-ur/a` — result of an action.

Final `a` in forms ending in `-oria`, `-ura`, `-ada`, and similar patterns belongs to the complete suffixal pattern. It does not license attachment of a separate `-a` to any derivational stem ending in `-t`.

For other suffixes, follow their own attachment rules. For example:

- `-ad/a` is used with verbs ending in `-ar`: `babillar → babillada`;
- `-id/a` is used with verbs ending in `-ir` or `-er`: `audir → audida`, `currer → currida`;
- with `-ment`, normally only final `r` or `n` is removed rather than using a `-t` stem: `guvernar → guvernament`, `mover → movement`.

Do not mechanically generate every theoretically possible derivative. The existence of a productive suffix does not mean that the derivative is naturally expected for the given meaning.

D. MANDATORY VALIDATION OF EVERY PROPOSED NOUN

Before including a form in `analysis.derivationalPotential` or `derivation.possibleDerivations`, determine:

1. whether it is a simple noun or a suffixed derivative;
2. whether it is based on the present stem, root, or derivational stem;
3. which ending or complete suffix is used;
4. whether a normative rule permits that combination;
5. whether the form coincides with the past tense or another grammatical form of the verb;
6. whether its meaning matches the function of the suffix;
7. whether the derivative is semantically natural rather than merely formally possible.

In `analysis.derivationalPotential`, name the principal nouns without derivational suffixes first. List suffixed nouns separately and do not present them as equivalent variants of one simple noun.

Do not use one list such as “nouns: X, Y, Z” when it mixes a simple noun, a bound stem, a past-tense form, and a suffixed derivative. Separate these types explicitly.

Do not list a form unless its morphological segmentation can be explained. Internally validate every form using this model:

`source verb → selected stem + ending/complete suffix → part of speech → meaning`.

You must not:

- present a bound derivational stem as an independent noun;
- present a past-tense form as a noun;
- attach a separate `-a` to a `-t` stem;
- invent suffixes, endings, or their meanings;
- mix the rule for a direct noun with the rule for a suffixed derivative;
- list every formally possible derivative without checking whether it is natural.

If no derivatives can be justified reliably, return an empty array. An empty array is preferable to invented or doubtful forms.
